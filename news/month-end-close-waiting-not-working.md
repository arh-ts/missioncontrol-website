# The month-end close is slow because work waits, not accountants

> Most close days are spent waiting for late inputs, approvals and rework, not on accounting work. Map the close as a dependency network and fix the flow before buying software or AI.

Published 23 November 2026 by the Mission Control team. Canonical: https://missioncntrl.io/news/month-end-close-waiting-not-working

## Key points

- APQC's open benchmark puts the median time to complete monthly consolidated statements at 6 calendar days. That figure counts elapsed time, including waiting.

- When finance teams are asked what slows the close, the most common answer is waiting: for data, for other departments, for systems. Few blame the speed of the accounting work itself.

- Most close data comes from vendor surveys with small or undisclosed samples. Treat the percentages as directional, but the pattern is consistent across a decade of them.

- AI and close software only shorten the close if they act on the critical path. Automating a step that was never the constraint changes little.

- Map the close as a dependency network, measure waiting against working at each step, find the critical path, then fix hand-offs before buying anything.

Ask a controller why the close takes eight days and you rarely hear "because reconciliations take eight days of effort." You hear that payroll was late, a project manager had not approved the accrual, the bank feed broke, or the partner who signs off was travelling. The accountants were ready. The inputs were not.

That distinction matters because most fixes on the market are aimed at the wrong problem. Close management software, reconciliation tools and now AI agents promise to make accounting work faster. In most services organisations the accounting work is not where the days go. They go in the gaps between steps.

This applies to in-house finance teams and to accounting firms running monthly close or bookkeeping for clients.

## How long the close takes, and what the number hides

The most widely used benchmark comes from APQC. Across 10,198 organisations, its [median cycle time to complete monthly consolidated financial statements is 6.0 days](https://www.apqc.org/resources/benchmarking/open-standards-benchmarking/measures/cycle-time-days-complete-monthly). The definition is worth reading closely: calendar days, including weekends, from the first trial balance to agreed consolidated statements. For finance shared services centres, APQC's [median to complete the monthly close is 8.0 days](https://www.apqc.org/what-we-do/benchmarking/open-standards-benchmarking/measures/cycle-time-days-finance-shared) across 3,123 organisations.

These are elapsed times. They include every hour a reconciliation sat untouched, waiting for a statement or a sign-off.

Vendor surveys tell a similar story with more spread. A 2025 benchmark from Ledge, a finance software company, surveyed 100 finance professionals and found [half of teams take six or more business days](https://ledge.co/content/month-end-close-benchmarks-for-2025), with only 18 per cent closing in three days or less. A May 2026 report from LiveFlow, another vendor, found [16 per cent of respondents take more than 10 days](https://www.cfodive.com/news/cfo-push-faster-month-end-close-stalled-data-bottlenecks-ai/819283/). LiveFlow did not disclose its sample size. Both firms sell close tooling, so read the numbers as indicative, not definitive.

## Where the days actually go

The more useful question is why. The answers line up.

- **Late inputs from other teams.** In the LiveFlow report, about [80 per cent blamed close delays on waiting for data from other systems or departments](https://www.cfodive.com/news/cfo-push-faster-month-end-close-stalled-data-bottlenecks-ai/819283/). Ledge found [56 per cent cited dependence on other departments and regions](https://ledge.co/content/month-end-close-benchmarks-for-2025), the top cause, and quoted one manager: "if even one source is delayed, it pushes back the entire close."

- **This is not new.** In 2017 the Journal of Accountancy reported an Adra survey in which [54 per cent named coordinating and getting information from other departments](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2017/may/overtime-stress-common-during-month-end-close-201716585.html) as a challenge. A FloQast survey of 259 close owners in the same article found 71 per cent relied on undocumented "tribal knowledge."

- **Reconciliation rework.** Ledge respondents named reconciling bank, card and processor accounts as the most frequent time sink. LiveFlow found more than half cite reconciling across multiple platforms as a barrier.

- **Batching at period end.** EY's record-to-report guidance describes work that is [heavily manual and concentrated at period end](https://www.ey.com/en_sg/insights/financial-accounting-advisory-services/how-to-transform-record-to-report-to-drive-strategic-value), with critical inputs often not complete and accurate until late in the period. It also warns that treating the close as separate tasks hides "interdependencies, bottlenecks and inefficiencies."

- **Approval queues.** Ledge ranked departmental submissions and approvals fifth among time-consuming tasks.

None of these is about how fast an accountant works. They are about when work can start and how long finished work waits.

### For accounting firms running client close

The pattern is sharper when the inputs belong to a client. In a 2026 report commissioned by HubSync, a vendor to CPA firms, [half of respondents said chasing documents was the operational process consuming the most staff time](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/?p=193690). Financial Cents' survey of 816 accounting, bookkeeping and tax firm owners found [getting documents from clients was still the biggest workflow issue](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/report-says-workflow-remains-top-120429546.html). For a bookkeeping practice, the client is the upstream department that is always late.

## Why the close behaves like a queue

A close is a network of dependent steps. Accruals depend on approved timesheets. Revenue recognition depends on billing. Intercompany depends on both sides posting. Review depends on preparation. The close finishes when the longest chain of dependent steps finishes. That chain is the critical path.

Two consequences follow. First, speeding up a step that is not on the critical path does not shorten the close at all. Second, on most critical paths the elapsed time is dominated by waiting. We covered the general mechanics in [our piece on flow efficiency](https://missioncntrl.io/news/flow-efficiency-services-firms): work spends most of its life queued, and queues grow sharply when the people they wait on are near full capacity. Period end is exactly when everyone is near full capacity.

## Why buying software or AI first rarely fixes it

Finance teams are investing. Gartner's 2025 survey of 183 finance leaders found [59 per cent of finance functions use AI](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-11-18-gartner-survey-shows-finance-ai-adoption-remains-steady-in-2025), but 91 per cent reported low or moderate impact so far. In September 2026 Gartner reported, from a survey of 160 finance leaders, that [low AI literacy is now the most significant barrier](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-09-24-gartner-says-cfos-must-take-a-more-disciplined-approach) and urged more deliberate portfolio management of AI spend.

LiveFlow's respondents said AI is mostly applied to analysis and reporting, leaving the manual core of the close largely unchanged. That fits the queue view. An AI tool that drafts variance commentary in minutes saves real effort, but if commentary was waiting two days for the final trial balance, the close still ends on the same day. Automation helps most when it removes a wait on the critical path. We have written about [why automation programs stall](https://missioncntrl.io/news/why-automation-programs-stall) when this sequencing is skipped.

## The talent squeeze makes waiting more expensive

The obvious response to a slow close, adding people, is getting harder. The AICPA's 2025 Trends report found [accounting bachelor's and master's degrees fell 6.6 per cent to 55,152](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2025/oct/the-accounting-graduate-pipeline-where-do-things-stand/) in 2023 to 2024, though enrolment rose 12.4 per cent in spring 2025. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects [about 115,300 openings a year](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/accountants-and-auditors.htm) for accountants and auditors from 2025 to 2035. In Australia, a CA ANZ survey of 159 members found [only 49 per cent of general accountant vacancies were filled](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/business/22301-ca-anz-survey-indicative-of-australia-wide-accountant-auditor-shortages), taking 79 days on average.

When skilled hours are scarce, an hour lost waiting is more costly. Fixing flow is the cheapest capacity a finance team can find.

## A framework: map the close as a dependency network

- **List every close step and its inputs.** For each step, name who supplies what, and from which system. Include external inputs: payroll provider, bank, client, project leads.

- **Draw the dependencies.** Which steps cannot start until another finishes? This is your network.

- **Measure waiting and working per step.** For three recent closes, record when each step could have started, when it actually started, the hands-on hours, and when it finished.

- **Find the critical path.** Trace the longest chain of dependent elapsed time from period end to sign-off. Only steps on this path set your close date.

- **Rank waits on the critical path.** The largest waits, not the largest tasks, are where to start.

**Illustrative example.** A 300-person consultancy closes on working day 7. Its critical path runs: timesheet approval (2 days elapsed, 1 hour of work), unbilled revenue accrual (1.5 days, 4 hours), management review (2 days, 3 hours), partner sign-off (1.5 days, 1 hour). Of about 7 working days on the path, roughly 9 hours are hands-on work. Flow efficiency on the critical path is under 20 per cent. A tool that halved accrual effort would save 2 hours. Moving timesheet approval deadlines to the last working day would save up to 2 days.

## Five fixes to try before buying anything

- **Pull inputs forward.** Set cut-offs for upstream teams before period end, and agree them as a service level, not a favour. For client close, request documents on a fixed schedule rather than one at a time when needed.

- **Reconcile continuously.** Move bank and card reconciliations to weekly or daily so period end only handles the last few days. This is the practical core of continuous accounting.

- **Shrink approval queues.** Set materiality thresholds below which a delegate can approve, and give approvers a fixed daily window during close.

- **Start steps on partial data.** Many accruals and reviews can begin with estimates and be trued up, so they stop waiting for the last input.

- **Write down the tribal knowledge.** Document who supplies each input and what "done" means.

Then re-measure. Once the waits are out, the remaining work is visible, and that is the right moment to decide which steps are worth automating. This is the kind of mapping Mission Control does: measuring how close work moves between people and teams, without reading its contents.

## Questions

### How many days should the month-end close take?

APQC's open benchmark puts the median time to complete monthly consolidated statements at 6 calendar days, based on more than 10,000 organisations. A better target is your own critical path with its avoidable waits removed, since organisational complexity varies widely.

### What is the critical path of a financial close?

It is the longest chain of dependent steps from period end to final sign-off. Only delays on this chain move the close date, so improvement effort should start there rather than on whichever task takes the most effort.

### Will AI shorten our month-end close?

Only if it removes waiting or rework on the critical path. Gartner's 2025 survey found most finance AI users reported low or moderate impact so far, so map where your close waits before deciding what to automate.
